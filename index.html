Visitor’s Guide

what this place is, and every key and switch in it

LUMIÈRE is an endless gallery. Its halls are laid as you walk, and every artwork is painted into being the moment you approach it — seeded, singular, shown to you alone. There is no goal and no far wall: wander, inspect what stops you, and save what you would keep. The Curator’s Office hangs your own works among the rest.

Sharing
Your gallery is private until you say otherwise: sign in in the office, claim a name, and switch on anyone with the link — switch it off to make it yours alone again.
The boundary
You walk an endless museum: halls are laid as you go, without asking. Anyone following your shared link does not — a guest walks exactly the rooms you curated and finds shut doors where the hanging ends, so nothing multiplies behind their backs. Close the boundary in the office to stand in the same walls a visitor does; a shut door then asks before any new room is born, and opening the switch again gives you back the halls without number.
By hand
On a phone or a tablet there is no cursor to lock, so the museum answers to three gestures and no more: drag anywhere to look around, lean the ring at the lower left to walk — the further you lean it the quicker you go — and tap a work to see it larger. A tap on the ring itself is a jump. Everything else is a switch you can already press — and once the office is open to you, a hang here pill appears under the reticle whenever you face a frame, which is H and U for a hand that has neither.
The switches at the lower right — lights, shutters, music, rain, curator — answer to touch as well as keys. The ? at the upper left opens this guide from anywhere; the ← beside it returns you to the entrance — press Esc first to free the cursor, or just press Esc twice.
